The pulse · Sweden in international context

Across the EU, firm AI adoption is climbing fast: the average reached 20% of enterprises in 2025, up from 8% in 2023 (Eurostat). Sweden is well above, at 35%, and its workforce is among the most exposed to generative AI of 36 countries; the placing depends on where the line is drawn, from 2nd at the quarter cut to 5th at a top-20% cut. The series that moves every month is the Swedish depth cut below, the share of vacancies asking for an AI skill, 2006–2026, now about 1.21% (2026, provisional), roughly 24× its level twenty years ago.

International benchmark (AI Index / Lightcast, 2025): AI skills are required in a median 1.9% of postings across 22 countries; Sweden 2.8% on that measure. Not directly comparable to the Swedish JobTech series above (a narrower, live measure).

In focus · Barriers: why AI is not used yet

The obstacle firms name most often is people, not money: 5.3% of Swedish enterprises say a lack of relevant expertise stops them using AI, against 1.2% who say AI is simply not useful to them. Cost ranks low. Shares are of all enterprises, not of non-adopters.
